Complete program

What’s included

A full year of core Grade 5 science: twelve interactive lessons, the student artifacts they produce, the assessments that check the learning, and the materials a facilitator needs to run it without a science background.

Inside one lesson

What a single lesson actually contains.

Every lesson follows the same five-layer structure, so once your student has done one, they know how to do all twelve.

1 Entry

A narrative problem that gives the student a real role — response team member, safety analyst, orbital intern — and a reason the science matters. The lesson opens with a situation, not a topic.

2 Exploration

The student chooses which evidence to pursue. Different paths surface genuinely different data, images, and observations — not different wording around the same content.

3 Sensemaking

Interactive tools let students manipulate the science directly: build a feedback loop model, adjust orbital variables, compare trait patterns across family groups, test a force-and-motion model.

4 Application

Students commit to a recommendation and defend it with the evidence they actually gathered. Decisions carry consequences within the story.

5 Reflection

A closing debrief asks how the student’s thinking changed and what evidence changed it — then everything exports as a field journal.

The complete package

Everything included with access.

A For the student

  • 12 interactive lessonsOne for every Arizona Grade 5 performance expectation, each 60–75 minutes of asynchronous work.
  • Field journal exportA formatted document capturing evidence notes, the path chosen, models built, written explanations, and reflections. Print it, file it, or add it to a portfolio.
  • Rigorous extensionsDeeper tasks in every lesson for students who finish early or want to push further into the science.
  • Progress savingWork is saved in the browser, so a lesson can be split across two sittings.
  • Read-aloud supportText-to-speech on reading cards for students who benefit from hearing the content.
  • GlossaryIn-lesson definitions for key science terms, available at any point without leaving the story.

B For the facilitator

  • Lesson-at-a-glanceOne page per lesson: the standard, time needed, the student product, and any materials.
  • Standards alignment guideArizona and NGSS codes with the Science and Engineering Practice and Crosscutting Concept for each lesson.
  • Answer guidanceWhat a strong response looks like — written to support conversation, not to mark a single correct answer.
  • Debrief promptsA few purposeful questions to help your student explain what they noticed and why the evidence supports it.
  • Short mastery quizA focused check on whether the student can apply the core ideas, not just recall them.
  • Pacing guideSuggested sequences for a full year, a semester, or a focused unit — with separate ordering for Arizona and NGSS.
  • Progress trackerA simple record of which lessons are complete and which artifacts have been saved.

C Technical requirements

  • Any modern browser on a Chromebook, laptop, or tablet
  • No installation, no accounts for students, no software to maintain
  • Student work saves locally in the browser on their own device
  • An internet connection matters most at the start and end of a lesson
  • Best experienced on a screen larger than a phone
Being straight with you

What this is not.

Knowing what a program doesn’t do is as useful as knowing what it does.

Not a daily curriculum

Twelve deep lessons plus extensions, not 180 days of worksheets. Most families run one lesson a week or two and add reading, experiments, and field experiences around it.

Not a graded course

There are no scores reported to you automatically and no gradebook. You get the student’s work and a short quiz; you decide what it means.

Not hands-off for the adult

The lesson carries the instruction, but the debrief conversation is where much of the learning consolidates. Budget ten to fifteen minutes.
