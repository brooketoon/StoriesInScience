Start to finish

How it works

From the moment you open a lesson to filing your student’s completed field journal. Roughly ninety minutes end to end, of which about fifteen involve you.

A single lesson

What one lesson looks like.

  1. Before — about 5 minutes

    You preview the lesson

    Open the lesson-at-a-glance page. It tells you the standard, the estimated time, what your student will produce, and anything you need on hand. You do not need to work through the lesson yourself, and you do not need a science background.

  2. Setup — about 2 minutes

    Your student opens the lesson

    Send them the link on a Chromebook, laptop, or tablet. There’s no login and no account. The lesson opens straight into the story.

  3. Lesson — 60 to 75 minutes

    Your student works independently

    They take on a role, choose which evidence to pursue, work with interactive tools, write in the field journal as they go, and commit to a recommendation they can defend. Progress saves as they work, so they can stop and return later.

  4. Export — about 1 minute

    They export the field journal

    At the end, the lesson generates a formatted document containing everything they wrote, the path they chose, the models they built, and their final reasoning. Save it, print it, or add it to a portfolio.

  5. Check — about 5 minutes

    Short mastery quiz

    A focused set of questions checks whether your student can apply the core ideas of the standard — not whether they can recall vocabulary.

  6. Debrief — 10 to 15 minutes

    You talk about the science

    Three or four prompts help your student explain what they noticed, why they decided what they decided, and how the evidence supports it. This is where much of the learning consolidates, and it’s the part worth protecting.

Across the year

How twelve lessons fit into a school year.

The program provides core instruction on each standard. How you space it is up to you.

One every two weeks

Twelve lessons across roughly 24 weeks. The most common pattern: a lesson one week, then reading, experiments, or a field trip on the theme the next.

Weekly science block

One lesson per week for twelve weeks as a focused science term, then move to another subject emphasis. Works well for co-ops that rotate subjects.

By domain

Group the life science lessons, then physical, then Earth and space. Useful if you’re coordinating with a reading list or a unit study.

Common questions about running it

Practical details.

? What if my student gets stuck?

The lesson scaffolds rather than gates. There are no wrong turns that end the investigation, no scores that lock, and no way to fail out. A student who chooses a less productive path still reaches the standard — that’s the design.

? Can they do the same lesson twice?

Yes, and it’s worth doing. Taking the other evidence path is a genuinely different experience, because the paths surface different data rather than different wording.

? Can two children share a device?

Work saves in the browser on that device, so it’s cleanest for each student to finish and export before the next begins. Separate browser profiles keep the work fully separate.

? What do I do with the exported journals?

Whatever your record-keeping requires. Families use them as portfolio evidence, as documentation for state reporting, or simply as a record of a year’s thinking. Co-ops and classrooms collect them as work samples.

? Do I need to grade anything?

No. The answer guidance describes what a strong response looks like so you can tell whether the reasoning landed, but nothing requires a score. The short quiz gives you a checkpoint if you want one.
