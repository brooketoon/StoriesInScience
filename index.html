Three-dimensional science learning

The standard is more than a fact students memorize.

Stories in Science deliberately integrates what students know, what scientists and engineers do, and the patterns of thinking that connect scientific ideas.

1

Science content

Students encounter the grade-level concepts inside a relevant problem rather than as isolated vocabulary or background reading.

2

Science & Engineering Practices

Students analyze information, develop and use models, construct explanations, support claims with evidence, and communicate decisions.

3

Crosscutting Concepts

Students use ideas such as patterns, cause and effect, systems, and stability and change to make sense of what the evidence shows.

Research-informed design

Every design choice has a purpose.

Stories in Science combines established learning and motivation theories with three-dimensional science instruction. The story, student choices, evidence, and reflection are not decorative extras—they work together to support how students learn and engage.

Constructivist Learning Theory

Students construct understanding.

Students connect new evidence with what they already know, investigate patterns, test ideas, revise their thinking, and construct explanations rather than simply receiving information.

Self-Determination Theory

Engagement is deliberately supported.

Meaningful choices support autonomy. Clear expectations, scaffolds, and feedback support competence. Authentic roles and problems help students feel connected to the work.

Interactive Narrative-Centered Digital Learning

The story organizes the science.

The narrative is not a reward added after instruction. The problem, evidence, decisions, and consequences create the environment through which students encounter and apply the science standard.

Theory becomes lesson design

Students experience the theory—not the terminology.

The framework translates research-informed principles into concrete lesson features that fifth graders can see and use.
